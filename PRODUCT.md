# Product

## Register

brand

## Platform

web

## Users

Written for two audiences who arrive by link — a shared essay, a referral, a
profile link — usually curious who made this and whether the work is real.

Primary: people who might hire or work with Alex — hiring managers, founders,
and collaborators sizing up product judgment and taste. Alex is a product
manager by trade with a design-led sensibility; here the craft is the audition,
standing in for a CV, and the essays and apps are the portfolio.

Secondary: fellow makers and the IndieWeb crowd — peers who notice a well-built
personal site, read for the craft, and pass it on.

The job a visitor is doing: gauge, in about a minute, whether this person has
real taste and judgment — enough to read further, subscribe, or reach out.

## Product Purpose

A personal home on the web built as a working notebook: long-form essays on
craft sitting beside the small software Alex ships (GRØD and other two-ink
tools). It exists to be a crafted, durable archive of work and thinking that
grows a readership, opens doors to product work and collaboration, and gives the
apps a home — with none of the growth-hacking machinery. Success is a visitor
who leaves convinced the person behind it has genuine taste and judgment, has
read something worth their time, and remembers the place.

## Positioning

The craft is the credential. Everything here is made to be looked at closely —
the essays, the small software, the site itself — because the making is the
argument. A working notebook that shows judgment instead of claiming it; the
work is the case for working together.

## Conversion & proof

- Primary and secondary CTA: read the writing (the essays are both the main draw
  and the main proof of judgment); secondarily, subscribe to the monthly
  notebook letter for readers not yet ready to reach out, with browsing the apps
  as a parallel path for the product-minded.
- The line a visitor remembers after 10 seconds: "Made with two inks."
- Belief ladder: (1) this person has real taste — the site proves it on sight;
  (2) this is earned, first-hand craft, not content marketing; (3) the work is
  real and actually ships; (4) I want to read more, follow, or work with them.
- Proof on hand: none yet — the writing and the shipped apps carry the
  credibility. No testimonials, client names, or press today; revisit a proof
  moment if named clients or app traction become available.

## Brand Personality

Quiet and precise, crafted and tactile, opinionated and essayistic, warm and
personal — four notes held at once. First person, dry-witted, generous but never
salesy. It should read like a person at a desk, not a brand: print-shop
metaphors, restraint as a method, and a stated allergy to products, funnels, and
growth-speak.

## Anti-references

Not a generic SaaS/startup landing (gradient hero, feature grid, "Get started
free"). Not a salesy funnel (pop-ups, urgency, subscribe-walls, growth hacks).
Not a sterile minimalist portfolio template (a cold Dribbble/Behance grid with
no point of view). Not loud or maximalist (busy, over-animated, look-at-me). The
throughline: nothing that trades craft or restraint for reach.

## Design Principles

The craft is the credential — every surface is itself the proof; earn trust by
being well-made, not by claiming to be.

Start from the constraint — solve with weight, size, and space before reaching
for more (the two-ink discipline as method, not decoration).

Show the work, skip the funnel — the writing and the apps do the persuading; no
pop-ups, urgency, or "get started free."

Commit to a signature — two inks, deliberately out of register; a specific,
distinctive identity beats safe neutrality.

A human at a desk — warm, first-person, generous; a notebook, not a marketing
site.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.1 AA. Preserve what already ships: honor prefers-reduced-
transparency and prefers-contrast: more (the blend and halftone drop to solid
ink, so meaning never depends on a blend mode), full light/dark support, and
status shown by dot plus label rather than color alone. Keep body text ≥4.5:1 on
the bone ground — the muted secondary ink is the value to watch. Motion is
limited to small hover transitions today; add a prefers-reduced-motion fallback
before introducing any entrance motion.
